About me

I am a Viera y Clavijo fellow at the Instituto de Astrofísica de Canarias (IAC) and the Universidad de La Laguna (ULL). My research focuses on the spectroscopic and dynamical study of massive stars and the star-forming regions where they are born, combining high-quality spectroscopy, Gaia astrometry and large spectroscopic surveys.

I lead MEIGAS, a research programme devoted to the multi-wavelength exploration of massive star-forming regions and OB associations in the Milky Way and beyond. Within the WEAVE-SCIP survey, I lead the OB-star component of the survey, co-lead SWAN (the high-resolution survey of Cygnus-X) and the Science Verification of massive stars, and I am the lead member of the SCIP Survey Working Group.

I obtained my PhD in Astrophysics at the IAC-ULL in 2019 (Massive stars in Cygnus OB2, supervised by Prof. Artemio Herrero), which received the Extraordinary PhD Award of the ULL. Since then, I have held postdoctoral positions at the Universidad de Alicante (Juan de la Cierva fellow), Keele University (Margarita Salas fellow), the ULL and the Centro de Astrobiología (CSIC-INTA), before returning to Tenerife in December 2025.

MEIGAS

Multi-wavelength Exploration of massIve star-forminG regions and ASsociations

Large spectroscopic surveys such as WEAVE and 4MOST are about to deliver spectra for an unprecedented number of massive stars. Combined with Gaia astrometry and photometry, they will make it possible, for the first time, to study massive stars and their birth environments in a statistically robust way.

MEIGAS is the research programme I lead to take advantage of this new landscape. Its goal is to carry out comprehensive, multi-wavelength studies of the major massive star-forming regions and OB associations of the Milky Way and nearby galaxies, in order to improve our understanding of:

  • how massive stars and their host associations form and evolve;
  • the poorly known evolutionary pathways of massive stars, including the role of rotation and binarity;
  • the dynamics of young stellar populations and the origin of runaway stars.

MEIGAS builds on my leading roles in WEAVE, my experience in the analysis of massive stars, and a collaboration network spanning several of the main surveys of massive stars (WEAVE, 4MOST, Gaia-ESO, XShootU, BLOeM).

Presented at IAU Symposium 402 (2025): Berlanas, Massive stars in the era of large spectroscopic surveys: the MEIGAS project.

Research

My research combines quantitative spectroscopy of massive stars with Gaia astrometry to understand how massive stars form, evolve and interact with their environment. My main lines of work are:

  • Massive star-forming regions and OB associations: Cygnus OB2 and Cygnus-X, the Carina Nebula (Car OB1) and Cas OB5.
  • Rotation of massive stars: the origin of slow and fast rotators, and its link with binary evolution.
  • Runaway stars and Galactic dynamics: what OB stars reveal about the kinematics of the Galactic disc.
  • Large spectroscopic surveys: preparation and scientific exploitation of WEAVE, 4MOST and Gaia-ESO.

Recent highlights

The Galactic rotation curve traced by young massive stars

Using more than 14,000 spectroscopically confirmed OB stars from the ALS III catalogue and Gaia DR3, we derived the rotation curve of the Milky Way between 5.5 and 11 kpc with the least dynamically evolved tracer available. The stars departing most from the curve form the largest sample of OB runaway candidates compiled to date.

Berlanas et al. 2026, submitted to A&A

The lack of fast rotators in Cygnus OB2

A spectral reclassification of the B0 population of Cyg OB2, led by my PhD student, sheds light on why this massive association seems to lack fast-rotating stars.

Galán-Diéguez, Berlanas et al. 2025, A&A

Massive stars in the Carina Nebula

Within the Gaia-ESO Survey, I built a new census of OB stars in Carina and carried out the spectroscopic analysis of its O-star population.

Berlanas et al. 2023, A&A · Berlanas et al. 2025, A&A

Close massive binaries at low metallicity

The BLOeM survey has revealed a high fraction of close massive binaries in the Small Magellanic Cloud, where I lead the analysis of projected rotational velocities of the OB sample.

Sana et al. 2025, Nature Astronomy

The structure and star-formation history of Cygnus OB2

Combining spectroscopy and Gaia, we found evidence of multiple star-forming bursts in Cyg OB2 and disentangled its spatial substructure.

Berlanas et al. 2020, A&A · Berlanas et al. 2019, MNRAS

Collaborations

WEAVE (2019 – present)

WEAVE is the new wide-field multi-object spectroscopic facility of the 4.2-m William Herschel Telescope at the Roque de los Muchachos Observatory. Within the Stellar, Circumstellar and Interstellar Physics (SCIP) survey, led by Prof. Janet Drew:

  • Lead of the OB-star component of the SCIP survey: selection of the high-mass stellar population and coordination of its scientific exploitation.
  • Co-lead of SWAN, the WEAVE-SCIP high-resolution survey of Cygnus-X: target selection of massive-star candidates, observing strategy and preparation for early science.
  • Co-lead of the Science Verification of massive stars, and lead author of the first SCIP Science Verification paper, on Cygnus OB2 (to be submitted soon).
  • Lead member of the SCIP Survey Working Group (since 2019; a two-person team): collation of target lists and survey densities from the whole SCIP team (50+ members), catalogue creation and combination, tiling strategy, field construction, operational rehearsal and observing configurations.
  • Member of the SCIP Quality Assurance Group, validating data quality and designing evaluation procedures for the spectroscopic pipeline.
  • Awarded WEAVE Survey Builder status (2021) in recognition of significant contributions to the preparation of the SCIP survey.

Other surveys and collaborations

  • Gaia-ESO Survey (2015 – present): Working Group 13, leading the analysis of the hottest stars of the survey.
  • BLOeM, Binarity at LOw Metallicity, ESO Large Programme (2024 – present): leading the projected rotational velocity analysis of the OB sample in the SMC.
  • XShootU, the ULLYSES and X-Shooter massive-star collaboration (2022 – present): Stellar Atmospheres and Benchmarking team.
  • 4MOST, 4MIDABLE-OB sub-survey (2022 – present): selection of OB candidates in the Carina region.
  • MEGASTAR, the MEGARA-GTC stellar spectral library (2018 – present): leading the analysis of the hottest stars.
  • GOSSS and the Alma catalogue of OB stars (ALS).
